Privacy

Your voice stays with you.

Sound Meter measures how loud you are. It never records audio, has no accounts, and keeps what it learns on your Mac. This page explains exactly what it does.

Last updated November 11, 2026

The short version

  • No audio is ever recorded, stored or sent anywhere.
  • Your history is a set of loudness numbers in a folder on your Mac. You can export or delete it at any time.
  • There are no accounts, no ads, and nothing is sold or shared with anyone.
  • The app is open source, so every claim here can be checked against the code.

Microphone

Sound Meter listens to your microphone to work out a single number: how loud the signal is right now. That number drives the orb. The audio itself is processed in memory and discarded immediately. It is never written to disk and never leaves your Mac.

History

Once a minute, Sound Meter saves a few numbers: how long it was listening, your average and peak level, how many seconds were too loud, and the name of the call app in use, if any. These are stored in ~/Library/Application Support/sound_meter/history on your Mac and removed automatically once they are 90 days old. Insights reads them to draw its charts. You can export them as a CSV file or clear them from the Insights window.

Call apps

To credit your loudness to the right place, Sound Meter asks macOS which apps are currently using a microphone. Only the app’s name (for example “Zoom”) is saved alongside each minute.

Calendar, if you connect it

Connecting your calendar is optional and off by default. If you turn it on, Sound Meter reads event titles, times and attendee names from the macOS Calendar app while Insights is open, to show how loud you were in each meeting. Calendar details are shown to you and then discarded. They are never saved by Sound Meter and never sent anywhere. You can turn this off from the menu or in System Settings.

Voice Focus

Voice Focus stops sound from your speakers counting toward your level. It is on unless you turn it off, and it only works if you say yes when macOS asks whether Sound Meter may hear what your Mac is playing. Say no and Sound Meter simply measures the microphone as before. That audio is used for one thing, as the signal to subtract from the microphone, and like the microphone audio it is processed in memory and discarded. It is never recorded, stored or sent anywhere. You can turn Voice Focus off in Settings, or withdraw the permission in System Settings.

Sharing, if you choose to

Nothing is shared unless you press Share. A share contains only the totals on the card: your calm percentage, time too loud, time monitored, calm streak, average level, and your loudest weekday and hour. It never includes meeting names, people, or anything from your calendar.

Share links carry those numbers in the link itself. The page they open is generated on the fly and nothing about it is stored, but anyone you send the link to can see the numbers in it. If you post to a social network, that network’s own privacy policy applies to your post.

Updates

Sound Meter checks GitHub for new versions about once an hour and downloads them from there. As with any download, GitHub can see your IP address and the request.

This website

The site is hosted on Vercel and uses Vercel Web Analytics to count page views. It does not use cookies or track you across sites. Fonts are loaded from Google Fonts, and the download buttons ask GitHub for the latest version, so those services see the usual request details such as your IP address. The “Try it with your voice” demo runs entirely in your browser; its audio is never recorded or sent.

Questions

Open an issue on GitHub and you’ll get a straight answer.
